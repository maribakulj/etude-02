# Mentem non formam plus pollere — "Mind, not form, prevails" *(prelim.)*

Editions: [A31b049](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b049) · [A34b048](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b048) · [A50a188](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a188) · [A21a189](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a189)

**Text.** A fox finds an actor's mask "so finely made that only the spirit was missing; in all else
it seemed alive", and says: "What a head — but no brain!" **Absence** (no brain), **appearance vs
reality** (seems alive), **speech**, and a contrastive negation in the motto.

**1531.** A fox holding **what looks exactly like a living human head**, eyes open. **1534.** The
fox holds a smaller head/mask by a fence, near a portico (the theatre store-room).

**Observation.** The text's very point — *it looks alive but is not* — is exactly what a picture
cannot settle: a well-drawn mask and a severed head are the same picture. The image is faithful to
the appearance and therefore cannot show the negation. Strong candidate for the category
"appearance vs being / absence of an inner property".

**1621 — correction of the observation above.** The fox holds a mask with **empty eye-holes**, and more masks hang on the wall: hollowness and the series of masks (the theatre store-room) settle "it is a mask, not a head". So appearance vs being is not undepictable as such; it needs **extra cues** (a mark of hollowness, a context of like objects) that the 1531 cutter did not supply. The inner absence (no brain) remains unshown.
