# Dulcia quandoque amara fieri — "Sweet things sometimes turn bitter"

Editions: [A31b088](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b088) · [A34b089](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b089) · [A50a111](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a111) · [A21a112](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a112)

**Text.** A "Lydian babe" strays from his mother and is stung by bees he thought harmless: "instead
of honey you give stings… without you, pain, no delight is granted." Change over time, mistaken
belief, negation, apostrophe to the bees.

**1531.** A **blindfolded Cupid with an arrow**, bees, and a woman holding an apple (Venus).
**1534.** Nude Venus, a child stung near hives. **1550.** A winged child, mother kneeling, hives.
**1621.** Mother and child; bees barely visible.

**Observation.** The text says "a Lydian babe"; the images name him **Cupid** (blindfold, arrow,
wings, Venus with apple) — the illustrator **particularises beyond the text** from the source
tradition (Theocritus' Cupid stung). Image adds identity; text keeps it generic.
