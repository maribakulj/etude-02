# In silentium — "On silence"

Editions: [A31b003](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b003) · [A34b003](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b003) · [A50a011](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a011) · [A21a011](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a011)

**Text.** Silent, the fool cannot be told from the wise; *therefore* let him press his lips, mark
silence with his finger, and **turn himself into** Egyptian Harpocrates. Condition (when silent),
causality (therefore), imperative/jussive, metamorphosis-by-comparison.

**All four editions.** A scholar in his study with a finger to (or hand on) his lips: standing
(1531, 1534), seated at the desk (1550, 1621). **Harpocrates never appears.** The image shows the
recommended act **as already performed**: the jussive mood becomes an indicative scene. The
fool/wise comparison, which is the argument, has no visual term.

**Verbal layers.** Iconclass image includes *Harpocrates* [96A33] for 1531 although no god is drawn
(text leakage, as A31b036).

**Role in the sample.** Control for a gesture-conventional motto (finger on lips = silence is a
stable visual sign): the image renders the *motto* well and the *epigram's argument* not at all.
