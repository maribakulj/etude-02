# In statuam Amoris — "On a statue of Love"

Editions: [A31b096](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b096) · [A34b097](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b097) · [A50a113](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a113) · [A21a114](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a114)

**Text.** A **refutation**: the poets say Love is naked, small, with arrows and wings, and blind —
"there is falsehood here": a blind archer, a wingless flyer, a boy older than Nestor make no sense.
Love is truly a pastime of idle hours; "its sign is a **Punic fruit (pomegranate) on a black
shield**". Negation of an image, argument, a new sign.

**1531.** A **blindfolded, winged boy with arrows** — exactly the image the text rejects.
**1534.** Winged naked boy, no bow, a shield (no fruit visible at this size). **1550.** Wingless
boy holding a dark shield. **1621.** Wingless boy, shield **with a pomegranate**.

**Observation.** The strongest case so far. A picture cannot say "Love is *not* like this"; the
1531 cutter **depicts the negated content**, which affirms it (Worth's thesis in action). Across
editions the image is **progressively corrected towards the text's positive sign** (wings and
blindfold dropped, pomegranate shield added by 1621). Negation is lost; what survives is the
replacement.
