# Cum larvis non luctandum — "Do not fight with ghosts" 

Editions: [A31b058](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b058) · [A34b057](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b057) · [A50a153](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a153) · [A21a154](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a154)

**Text.** Dying Hector **speaks**: "Tear me as you will; when the lion is dead, even cowardly hares
pluck his beard." Negative imperative in the motto; a speaker (Hector), a past (his victories), a
simile (lion / hares).

**1531 and 1534.** Hares around a lion. Neither shows Hector, Achilles, the chariot or the ropes.
The image depicts **only the vehicle of the simile**, not its tenor, and drops the speaker. In
1531 the lion's death is not evident; in 1534 the lion lies flat with a broken weapon by it,
which makes "dead" readable.

**Observation.** "Picture the simile, not the referent" is a recurring choice to check. It turns a
speech act into a fable scene and makes the negative imperative of the motto unreachable from the
image alone.

**1550 / 1621.** The lion lies limp (1621: on its back, legs in the air) with hares on and around it: death is made unambiguous by **posture**. Hector still absent.
