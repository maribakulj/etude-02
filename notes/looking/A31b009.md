# In victoriam dolo partam — "On a victory won by guile"

Editions: [A31b009](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b009) · [A34b009](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b009) · [A50a048](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a048) · [A21a048](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a048)

**Text.** First-person speech: "I, Virtue, bedew with tears the tomb of Ajax… that guile should
appear to have the better cause." A speaker who is an abstraction, a past event (the judgement of
arms), a counterfactual-tinged complaint.

**1531.** A woman tearing her hair beside an unlabelled tomb. **1534–1621.** The tomb is inscribed
**D M AIACIS**; the woman sits on it, tearing her hair (bare-breasted in 1621).

**Observation.** The inscription particularises the *tomb* (Ajax) but **nothing identifies the
woman as Virtue**: the image gives "a mourning woman". The speaker's identity, the past judgement
and the guile are lost. Second case of **inscription as the image's way back to proper names**
(A31b013, A31b039).
