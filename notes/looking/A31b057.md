# Firmissima convelli non posse — "The firmest cannot be uprooted" *(prelim.)*

Editions: [A31b057](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b057) · [A34b056](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b056) · [A50a042](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a042) · A21a: to see.

**Text.** Concessive and negative: *though* Ocean rouses his waves, *though* you, Turk, drink the
Danube dry, you *shall not* break through while Charles V leads; so oaks stand firm though winds
strip their leaves. Political tenor (Turks, Charles V), future, concession, negation.

**1531.** Two wind-heads blow on a bare tree. **1534 / 1550.** Wind-heads with visible blasts,
leaves (oak leaves and acorns in 1550) falling, trunk standing.

**Observation.** "Cannot be uprooted" is rendered by **co-presence of force and persistence**: the
blowing winds and the standing trunk. Negated outcome = the depicted absence of the effect, which is
only readable as negation because the force is shown. The entire political tenor is dropped (as in
A31b058, the image keeps the comparison's vehicle). 1531's bare tree loses the falling leaves that
show the wind *does* act on the periphery — the concession.
