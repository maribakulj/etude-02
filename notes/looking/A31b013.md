# Illicitum non sperandum — "Do not hope for what is not allowed" *(prelim.)*

Editions: [A31b013](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b013) · [A34b079](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b079) · [A50a046](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a046) · A21a: not looked at yet.

**Text.** Hope and Nemesis (Retribution) stand beside the poet's altar "so that you hope only for
what is allowed". A **prohibition** (negative imperative) and two **abstractions**.

**1531.** Two standing figures — one winged, with sword and bridle; the other holding a broken
arrow — and two children (one with bow and arrows). Nothing tells which figure is Hope and which
Nemesis; nothing expresses "not allowed".

**1534 Wechel.** Two statues on two pedestals inscribed **SPES** and **NEMESIS**. The image
identifies its abstractions **by writing their names inside the picture**: the word comes back into
the image where depiction cannot identify. **1550 Lyon.** Two figures again, no inscription visible
at this resolution; altar between them.

**Questions.** "Inscription inside the image" is a strategy to count. The prohibition itself is
not rendered in any edition seen: the pair Hope + Retribution side by side leaves the inference
(hope is checked by punishment) to the viewer.
