# In momentaneam foelicitatem — "On short-lived happiness"

Editions: [A31b068](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b068) · [A34b068](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b068) · [A50a124](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a124) · [A21a125](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a125)

**Text.** A gourd grew beside a pine, overtopped it and thought itself superior; the pine
**speaks**: "This glory is brief: winter will soon come and destroy you." Future, prediction,
speech, pride.

**1531.** A pine and **a gourd climbing its own pole beside it** — not enveloping the pine.
**1534–1621.** The gourd climbs the tree, fruit hanging.

**Observation.** The image shows the *present* (flourishing); the future (winter, destruction) that
gives the meaning is not shown in any edition. Pure case of Worth's "pictures depict the present".
1531 again misses the key configuration (envelopment).
