# Impossibile — "Impossible" (washing the Ethiopian)

Editions: [A31b083](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b083) ·
[A34b084](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b084) ·
[A50a059](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a059) · [A21a059](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a059)

**Text.** "Why are you washing an Ethiopian in vain? Oh, do stop. No one can turn the shades of
black night into light." A proverb (*Aethiopem lavare*); the meaning is **modal** (impossibility),
**negative** (in vain, no one can), addressed to someone (**apostrophe**, imperative "stop").
Note: a racialised proverb; Iconclass and Glasgow use period/dated terms. The article must handle
this explicitly and carefully.

**1531 woodcut (looked at).** A man lies on a bed; one man washes him with a sponge, another holds
a basin. **The washed man is not shown as dark** — he is left white like the others. The image
shows an ordinary washing; nothing marks the futility, nor even the premise on which the joke
depends.

**1534 / 1550.** The washed man is hatched dark; two or three men scrub him; well, jug, towel.
What the image can show is the *attempt*; "in vain" requires an outcome over time, which a single
frame cannot show. The image relies on the viewer knowing the proverb.

**Verbal layers.** Glasgow alt-text 1531: "Two white men washing a black man" and Iconclass image
1531 includes the ethnic notation — again the **text dictates the description of an image that
does not show it**. Iconclass "text": *Impossibility*, *Vanitas*, *day and night*.

**Questions raised.** Modality (impossible, in vain) and apostrophe/imperative look like clear
"cannot be drawn" categories. The image depicts the *situation* on which the modal operates and
lets the proverb do the rest — anchorage in Barthes' sense, but reversed in time: the text came first.

**1621.** A standing dark-skinned man washed by two men with cloth and jug. Same reading as 1534/1550: the attempt is shown, the futility is not.
