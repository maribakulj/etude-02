# In occasionem — "On Opportunity"

Editions: [A31b017](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b017) · [A34b016](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b016) · [A50a121](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a121) · [A21a122](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a122)

**Text.** An ekphrastic **dialogue** with a statue by Lysippus: question and answer for each
attribute — standing on points (always moving), winged feet, razor (keener than any edge), forelock
(to be seized as she comes), **bald back of the head** (once she has passed, she cannot be caught).
Time, condition ("if anyone lets me go…") and speech carry the whole meaning.

**1531.** A naked female figure, hair blown forward, holding a blade, drapery; standing on what may
be a sphere/wheel on water. **1534.** The same type, with a wind-head, a razor, winged ball or
wheel under her feet, a sail-like veil.

**Observation.** The image keeps the **attributes** and drops the dialogue. Each attribute is a
compressed piece of time or condition ("bald behind" = *after she has passed you cannot seize
her*). Attribute-as-condensed-narrative is a strategy distinct from omission; the image needs the
viewer to unfold it. The address to "you, stranger" disappears.

**1550.** Female figure on a wheel at sea, razor, veil. **1621.** The figure becomes **male**, standing on a ball, razor raised, forelock — closer to Lysippus' *Kairos* named in the text than to the Latin female *Occasio*. The drift goes **towards the text's source** rather than away from it (compare A31b096).
