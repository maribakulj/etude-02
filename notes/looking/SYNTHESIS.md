# S3 synthesis — what twenty emblems show

Sample: 20 emblems of the 1531 edition, each looked at in the four core editions (1531 Steyner,
1534 Wechel, 1550 Lyon, 1621 Padua), except A31b087 (no 1531 woodcut). Notes: one file per emblem
in this folder.

## How the sample was chosen (and its bias)

A crude cue count on Glasgow's English translations (negation, time, condition, speech, causality
words) pointed to candidates; the final choice was made by reading the texts, to cover: negation
in the motto (013, 036, 049, 057, 058, 083), time (012, 017, 039, 068, 088, 095), condition or
hypothesis (006, 059), speech/dialogue (009, 017, 039, 059, 068, 096), abstraction (006, 009, 013),
paradox of properties (053), colour and change (087), and three relatively concrete controls (003,
023, and the fable 049). **Bias:** the sample is deliberately rich in "undepictable" content; it
says nothing about frequencies. It is a design sample for categories, not an estimate.

## Recurrent operations (candidate categories, from the cases)

What the image does with content the text states but cannot be drawn:

1. **Omission** — the content simply disappears (futility in 083; winter to come in 068; the
   political tenor in 057; Hector's speech in 058). The default.
2. **Depict the vehicle, drop the tenor** — the comparison's image becomes the whole picture
   (hares and lion 058; oak and winds 057; vine and elm 012).
3. **State co-presence** — a negated or future outcome is shown as two contrasting states side by
   side: force + persistence (057), dead + living (012), mighty + flourishing-weak (068 partly).
4. **Contrast of two terms + deixis** — negation "not X but Y" as X and Y both shown, sometimes a
   pointing gesture (036 in 1534; 023's pointing for "lending eyes").
5. **Attribute as condensed narrative** — time/condition folded into an attribute the viewer must
   unfold (Occasio's bald nape 017; sceptre with crows 006).
6. **Naming inside the image** — inscriptions (SPES/NEMESIS 013 in 1534; D M AIACIS 009, 039).
   Alternative: **attributes** (bridle for Nemesis 013 in 1621). Not stable across editions.
7. **Mood shift** — imperative/jussive becomes the act performed (003: "let him press his lips" →
   a man with a finger on his lips).
8. **Depicting the negated content** — the picture shows what the text denies, thereby affirming
   it (096 in 1531: blind winged archer the poem refutes).
9. **Particularisation beyond the text** — the image names what the text keeps generic (088:
   "a Lydian babe" → Cupid with Venus), and the reverse, **generalisation** (095: Heraclitus and
   Democritus → two men).

What the image fixes that the text left open (or must commit to): number of figures, posture
(standing/seated, 003), setting (study, landscape, city), gender (017 becomes male in 1621),
material (metal vs clay pots, 059 — lost in 1621), species (chameleon 087, unknown to early cutters).

Medium-specific vs picture-generic limits: **colour** (087) is impossible in black-line woodcut
but not in pictures as such; **change over time** is impossible for any single picture. The scheme
must separate the two.

## Drift across editions

- **1531 departs from the text's key configuration** in at least 7 of 19: an empty shrine without Isis (036 — corrected, see note), the "Ethiopian" not dark (083), crown on the crow instead of crows on the sceptre (006),
  gourd on its own pole (068), lame and blind walking side by side (023), the refuted Cupid (096),
  ambiguous head/mask (049). This fits Steyner's own description of the cuts as rough signs and
  Hunger's account that cutters worked from descriptions. **The 1531 edition is a special case,
  not a representative illustrator.**
- Later editions often **converge towards the text and its sources** (096 pomegranate shield by
  1621; 017 male Kairos in 1621; 049 hollow mask in 1621) — but can also **lose** a text-relevant
  distinction (059 metal vs clay).

## The verbal layers of the catalogue

> **Correction after review 1 (S5):** the cases below are better explained by **record
> inheritance** between editions (A31b036's alt-text is A34b035's with the plural removed; 51/312
> later alt-texts are identical to 1531) than by cataloguers reading the text. See PROBLEM v0.2, H4.

- Glasgow's alt-text and Iconclass "image" notations for 1531 sometimes describe **the text, not
  the woodcut** (036 Isis; 083 skin colour; 003 Harpocrates).
- But not always: in 053 the alt-text follows the image ("a slug") against the text
  ("sucking-fish").
- Therefore the dependence of the catalogue's "image" layer on the text is **an empirical variable
  to measure**, and it is measurable here because the 1531 woodcuts often diverge from the text:
  where image and text disagree, we can see which one the cataloguer followed.

## Consequences for the problem (S4)

1. Worth's list (negation, condition, tense, questions) is a usable *a priori* frame, but the cases
   show it is incomplete: **speech/address**, **identity (proper names)**, **modality** (083),
   **properties not visible** (speed 053, material 059, inner absence 049), **comparison/analogy**
   are as important.
2. The interesting object is not only "what is lost" but **which operation** the illustrator uses,
   and whether the operation is **stable across illustrators** of the same text. The four-edition
   panel lets us measure agreement between illustrators on the same text — a natural replication.
3. The 1531–1534 disagreements give a **natural experiment for the catalogue and for models**:
   cases where the image does not show what the text says. A describer (cataloguer or model) that
   reports the text's content for such an image is reading the text, not the picture.
4. The sensitive racial content (083) must be handled explicitly in the article.
