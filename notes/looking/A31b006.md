# Concordia — "Concord" (crows)

Editions: [A31b006](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b006) · [A34b006](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b006) · [A50a038](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a038) · [A21a038](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A21a038)

**Text.** Crows live in unanimity; *for this reason* a sceptre carries these birds; leaders stand
by the people's consent; **if** consent is withdrawn, discord drags kings down. Causality,
condition, an abstraction, a generalisation.

**1531.** A crowned crow followed by crows: the cutter **transfers the crown from the sceptre to
the bird** — the image literalises "royal" on the wrong object. **1534–1621.** A sceptre topped
with a fleur-de-lis standing on a pedestal/altar, with two crows (billing in 1534; four birds
around in 1550/1621). The emblematic object is now right.

**Observation.** The conditional (withdraw consent → discord) is absent everywhere; concord itself
is rendered by **pairing/symmetry** of birds. The image keeps the *sign* (sceptre with crows) and
drops the *argument*.
