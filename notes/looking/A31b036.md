# Non tibi sed religioni — "Not for you but for religion"

Editions: [A31b036](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A31b036) ·
[A34b035](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A34b035) ·
[A50a007](https://www.emblems.arts.gla.ac.uk/alciato/emblem.php?id=A50a007) · A21a: to see.

**Text.** An ass carries an image of Isis; passers-by kneel and pray; the ass thinks the worship is
for him and swells with pride, until the driver beats him and says: "You are not a god, little ass,
you carry a god." The point is a **contrastive negation** ("not X but Y") delivered as **direct
speech**, and an **error of attribution** (the ass's false belief).

**1531 woodcut (looked at).** An ass, a man raising a club/mallet behind it, a robed figure in front
holding something. **The ass carries nothing**: no image of Isis, no shrine. An arched shape stands
behind it (a doorway or niche?). It is not clear that the front figure kneels. Without the carried
image the contrast that makes the meaning ("you / the god you carry") has no visual term at all.

**1534 Wechel.** A shrine with a statue is placed on the ass's back; several figures kneel at right;
the driver whips with one hand and **points with the other** — the pointing gesture is a possible
pictorial stand-in for the driver's speech (deixis: "that, not you").

**1550 Lyon.** More narrative: city gate, crowd, worshippers on the ground; the driver strikes
from behind. The pointing gesture is not obvious.

**Verbal layers.**
- Glasgow alt-text, 1531: "Figure kneeling before ass carrying image of goddess but being whipped
  by driver" — describes **the text**, not the 1531 woodcut (no image carried, kneeling unclear).
- Iconclass "image", 1531: *statues ~ objects*, *Egyptian religion*, *kneeling on both knees*,
  *worship and devotion* — the same: **the text has leaked into the image indexing**.
- Iconclass "text": Arrogance, Honour, Misuse — the moral, not the negation.

**Questions raised.**
1. The negation "not you but…" seems pictorially carried, when at all, by *contrast of two terms*
   plus *deixis* (pointing), not by any sign of negation. Check across the corpus.
2. **Method warning:** the cataloguer's "image" layer is not independent of the text. Any measure
   that treats Iconclass-image as "what the picture shows" will be wrong for cases like this one.
   We need our own image-only description, made without reading the text, for at least a sample.
